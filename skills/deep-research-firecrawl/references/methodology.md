# Evidence and synthesis

## Scope and search

Define the decision and inclusion criteria before retrieval: relevant populations, jurisdictions, dates, product versions, and outcomes. Distinguish “latest available now” from “what was known at a historical cutoff.” For technical questions, establish the current stable release and check the user's actual version; do not silently mix stable, prerelease, and unreleased documentation.

Search for primary evidence, alternatives, constraints, and counterexamples. Adapt breadth to the question. For literature reviews, record queries, search dates, filters, and databases; do not call an exploratory review systematic or exhaustive without a reproducible protocol and coverage assessment.

## Evidence ledger

Use this ledger for deep/ultradeep work; a compact equivalent suffices for shorter tasks:

| Claim | Source ID and passage/location | Provenance and date/version | Independent support or conflict | Assessment and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One narrowly stated claim | URL/DOI plus page, section, table, or quoted passage | Original producer; published/updated date; access date separately | Distinct origins, not duplicated reporting | Supported, provisional, disputed, or unresolved; explain why |

Keep short supporting excerpts or precise locations within copyright limits. Preserve the context needed to check a number: unit, denominator, geography, population, period, and method. For computed values, retain inputs, sources, formula, and assumptions. Never substitute a model-generated summary for source evidence.

## Source assessment

- **Authority:** Is this source responsible for or positioned to know this particular fact? Vendor docs establish their API behavior; independent evaluations are stronger evidence for comparative performance.
- **Method:** Are sampling, controls, measurement, uncertainty, and exclusions appropriate? Distinguish observational findings from causal evidence.
- **Provenance:** Trace secondary claims to the underlying paper, dataset, filing, or statement. Syndication, shared datasets, and common authorship limit independence.
- **Currency:** Match the source's date/version to the claim. Old foundational evidence can remain relevant; newer is not automatically better. Unknown dates stay unknown. Check corrections/retractions and material changes when applicable.
- **Incentives:** Identify sponsorship, promotional claims, conflicts of interest, and selective reporting. Explain the implication rather than applying a universal numeric score.

Do not require three citations for every claim. Independently corroborate contested or consequential findings where possible; state when only one origin is available. Cite authoritative primary documentation for a provider's own versioned contract.

## Synthesis and counterevidence

For each finding, separate the source-backed statement, interpretation, decision implication, and uncertainty. Explain disagreements using differences in definitions, versions, timing, methods, or populations. If those differences do not resolve the conflict, preserve it.

For deep/ultradeep work, ask what evidence would change the conclusion and search for it deliberately. Test whether plausible changes to key assumptions reverse the recommendation. Do not claim quantitative confidence without a defensible method.

Stop when the decision-relevant questions are covered and further searching yields little independent evidence, or the time/cost/access constraints are reached. State gaps and their consequences. Suggest focused follow-up only where it could change the decision.

## Final human/agent review

- Verify each material claim against the passage cited, including nearby qualifiers.
- Ensure the bibliography describes sources actually consulted; disclose abstract-only or secondary access.
- Check that recommendations follow from evidence and the user's constraints.
- Include counterevidence and retrieval limitations; do not bury unresolved disagreements.
- Use automated checks for report structure and link metadata, never as a certificate of factual accuracy.
